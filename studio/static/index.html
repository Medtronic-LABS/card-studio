    Try a card with a model

    Pick a card (its template and grid align and cut your photo) and a model to read it with. A pack or run reader uses the card's field rules and grammars; an outside model reads greedily in its own characters. A model made for another card can read poorly: that is what this shows. Nothing is sent anywhere; the photo is not kept.
    A pack is a template and a reader together, so adding one gives you both: its id shows up in the Card list and in the Model list. An id and version already installed is never overwritten.

    How to use from a new card to a model on the phone

    1. Add the card type open Cards →
      Under Add a new card type, drop one clean, flat picture of the card. Use the same kind of capture as the cards you will read later: a scan for scans, a phone photo for phone photos. Turn it the right way up, fix the detected cells, and give a name to the cell that labels each field (the label at the left of a row, or the heading above a column). Approve and save the template.
    2. Save its grid open Cards →
      In Layouts, press Grid. Say whether each field is a row (a column per visit, like ANC and EPI cards) or a column (a row per visit, like the NCD card). Skip printed label columns and heading rows until the boxes in the preview sit exactly on the cells people write in, then save.
    3. Add filled cards open Cards →
      In Filled scans, leave the card on Auto-identify and add your scans, photos or a PDF. Each one is matched to its template, straightened and cut into cells. Open Scans and press View grid on anything marked check. A scan that matches no template is refused, not guessed. Give each batch a writer code (W07 or a clinic id, never a name): with codes for at least five writers, training is tested on people it never saw.
    4. Set each field's kind open Label →
      Pick the card, then choose a kind for every field: number, date, bp, mark or text, and an expected range for numbers. Do this before labeling: the kind decides which characters the reader may produce. A date written on two lines (day.month over the year) or a blood pressure either side of a printed diagonal has a layout to set as well: the cell is then split into two pieces, each read as one line. You still label the whole value, such as 22/2/26 or 120/80.
    5. Label the cells open Label →
      Press Label on a field and type what each cell says. Enter saves and moves on, Ctrl+B means nothing is written, Ctrl+U means it cannot be read. Training needs labels on at least 10 pages; more pages and more writers give a better reader. Once the card has been trained, press Read the cells with AI: the card's own reader fills in what it is sure of and you only confirm or correct, and the queue can put the cells it is least sure of first. To share the work, send the cells to Label Studio from the strip below the table and pull the labels back.
    6. Train and check open Train & Ship →
      Choose the card and press Train + gate + export. Every labeled cell is read once by a model that never saw its page. A field ships only if the low end of its 95% interval meets the target; the rest stay needs-review. Below it, Is there enough labeled data? says for each field whether it only needs more checked cells (and about how many) or is not accurate enough yet, and can measure a learning curve.
    7. Take the model open Cards →
      In Model registry, press View on the new pack. Download the pack (template, grid, field rules, and the reader as ONNX and TensorFlow Lite), or only reader.tflite.
    8. Improve it open Model quality →
      Look at which fields and which mistakes are worst, label more of those, train again, and compare the two runs in History.
    Everything runs on this machine. Large batches and Label Studio start from the command line: python studio/scans.py add auto FOLDER, python studio/labelstudio.py start.
